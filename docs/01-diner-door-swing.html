No handle, no latch, no lock. The design follows entirely from one fact: you arrive at this door with both hands full and open it with a hip or a shoulder. Every piece of hardware on it exists to survive that, and the porthole exists because you can't stop — you need to see who's coming before you hit it.

1

Hand rub, beside the plate

The worn patch is next to the push plate, not on it. People miss the plate — they shove the bare wood. It polishes lighter, not darker, because the finish wears off.

2

Smudge halo

Fingerprints ring the bezel where people steady themselves while looking through.

3

Grime band

A dark line sits directly above the kick plate — mop water and grease stop at the metal edge and dry there. The sharpest tell in the whole photo.

4

Boot scuff

Heaviest at the leading corner, furthest from the pivot, where the foot actually lands.

5

Hazed stainless

Never mirror. Fine omnidirectional scratching kills reflection — a polished chrome shader here will look wrong instantly.

5 · Build notes

Cut the porthole as real geometry, not an alpha card — players will use it as a sightline and a flat texture breaks the moment anyone approaches off-axis. Collision stays a plain box; never trace the round hole.

The leaf wants a hinge constraint with a spring return and ±95° limits, not a scripted animation, so it takes an impulse from a body running into it and settles on its own. That overshoot-and-settle is most of what sells the door.

Two honest flags. The photo is shot at an angle, so every proportion here is estimated off it rather than measured — a square-on shot, or one measurement of the leaf width, would let me replace the lot. And I could not reach the s&box docs from this session to name the exact hinge component, so the mechanism above is described by behaviour rather than by API.
Scaled up from reality. A real commercial swing door is about 36 × 84. This one is 48 × 96, because the Source player hull is 32 wide and a true-size door leaves four inches a side — players catch on the jamb constantly. It reads slightly chunkier than the photograph, and that is the correct trade.
